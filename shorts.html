Longer summary

What Muddy Waters says

The report is 54 pages, titled "Edison International Should be Liable for ~$20 Billion." Muddy Waters says it is short Edison International (EIX), parent of Southern California Edison, and intends to begin covering a substantial majority, possibly all, of its short positions on publication, a risk-management step it says does not reflect lower conviction. Its thesis is that newly assembled evidence from Edison's own regulatory filings shows the utility consciously disregarded public safety when it failed to consider de-energizing the four 220-kV transmission circuits running through Eaton Canyon (Eagle Rock-Mesa, Goodrich-Gould and two Mesa-Vincent circuits) on the evening of 7 January 2025. Los Angeles County fire investigators concluded on 4 August 2026 that electrical arcing on an idle Edison tower in that corridor started the fire, which killed 19 people. Muddy Waters argues the ignition was foreseeable and preventable.

The evidence offered

  • A known model defect. Edison's Fire Potential Index (FPI), the main tool deciding which circuits come into scope for a public safety power shutoff, assigned its maximum wind value to sustained winds of 29 mph. Muddy Waters says Edison's 2022 Wildfire Mitigation Plan shows Edison saw this deficiency in 2019 when it began using the model, promised a replacement ("FPI 2.0") and apparently defunded it after 2023. After the fire Edison said its model could not "capture extreme wind events."
  • The circuits were probably never scored. The report says Edison overrode FPI for circuits it was watching but seemingly never scored the Eaton Canyon transmission circuits, and that a second tool, FireCast, was never consulted because it requires the FPI threshold to be met.
  • A recloser that could not be disabled. Edison's 6 February 2025 incident report to the CPUC says an attempt to disable automatic reclosing on the adjacent Eagle Rock-Gould circuit was unsuccessful. Two former San Diego Gas & Electric operators told Muddy Waters a line under a Particularly Dangerous Situation Red Flag Warning "should be on zero shot," and that breakers can generally be opened quickly and remotely. Surveillance video shows two flashes about three seconds apart at the ignition point, matching two faults in Edison's records.
  • Little cost to customers. At 6 p.m. Edison's demand was about 12,000 MW against estimated winter capacity of about 20,000 MW, and the circuits tripped out anyway between about 7 p.m. and 12:30 a.m. Edison de-energized all four circuits on 12 and 20 January 2025 with no customer outages, and a former Edison operations executive said de-energizing a circuit without affecting the grid is routine.
  • Induction risk Edison already recognized. The leading theory is induction from an energized circuit to an idle line out of service since 1971. Edison's own standard treats induction as a hazard wherever lines run parallel for over 600 feet; Muddy Waters measures the parallel spans at about 1,150, 1,800 and 2,600 feet. It points to the 2018 Kramer Junction contractor death, for which the CPUC cited Edison $4.5 million.
  • Context on 7 January. Edison knew the Palisades Fire was already burning, regional suppression resources were committed, and Los Angeles had declared a state of emergency an hour before ignition. Edison had told a CPUC workshop that shutoffs could have cut structures damaged by its past large fires by more than 90%.
  • Governance. CEO Pedro Pizarro's pay rose 19.8% to about $16.5 million in 2025 with no safety deduction, and the covered-conductor target was cut to 650 miles in February 2025, below the 813 built the year before.
  • Liability math. Under California's wildfire fund rules, a finding of "conscious or willful disregard" removes the reimbursement cap. Muddy Waters estimates about $20 billion (about $20 a share, discounted and post-tax, using 384.8 million shares) in that case and about $4.3 billion (about $6.50 a share) if Edison is found imprudent, which it calls near certain.

Position, stock reaction, company response

  • Firm's position: short, per the report's disclosure, with an intention to cover most or all of it on publication.
  • Stock: EIX opened at $56.04 and closed at $55.48, down 1.0%, per Activ8 Insights. Insurance Journal and Carrier Management, citing Reuters, reported the shares fell more than 3%. Market capitalization was roughly $21 billion at about 385 million shares. By 13 November the stock was at $60.18, up 7.4% from the report-day open.
  • Company: Edison's formal regulatory position, quoted in the report, is that it "will be able to make a good faith showing" that its conduct in the area of origin was consistent with a reasonable utility. Edison has countersued Los Angeles County, Pasadena Water and Power, five other water agencies and Southern California Gas over evacuation warnings, firefighting water and gas shutoffs.

Where the case is weakest or unsupported

  • The roughly $20 billion figure is Muddy Waters' own estimate. The report notes that its reference points, such as Milliman's $13.7 billion to $22.8 billion estimate, measure insured loss, not utility liability, and the payment schedule and 8% to 9% discount rate are Muddy Waters' assumptions.
  • The decisive question, whether Edison acted with willful disregard, is a determination by the California Wildfire Fund Administrator that has not been made. Muddy Waters itself says imprudence, not willful disregard, is the more certain outcome.
  • Induction from an idle line is described as "the leading theory" of the cause, not a final finding, and the argument that shutting the circuits would have caused no extra outages rests partly on a former executive's estimate of winter capacity.
  • Several points depend on interpretation of Edison's own filings and on unnamed former operators, whose views on what "should" have been done are opinion, not the regulatory standard.
